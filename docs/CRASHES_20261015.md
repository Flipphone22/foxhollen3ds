## Audio lifetime repair, subsequent continuation

The actual PC sample registry, decoder and mixer now reproduce a use-after-free
under ASan. Removing a bank's final registration frees its uploaded bytes, but
`hwBreak` can schedule the stop at a later subframe. The mixer reads those bytes
before reaching that stop. A voice from another macro can also remain active
without a scheduled break. Nine synthetic cases reproduce the read; the
immediate-break control case does not. This is a confirmed lifetime defect,
not a reproduction establishing the cause of OS dump194.

The native source adapter now retires actual consumers at final sample release:
it matches active DSP voices by allocation address, removes their synth ownership,
and synchronously calls the existing hardware-off path before freeing storage.
That path preserves the existing detached PCM fade-out tail. Shared registrations
are retained, unrelated voices continue, and the allocation is checked again
after stop callbacks so a replacement voice is not stopped accidentally.
The caller's existing recursive synth lock still provides serialization.
There is no new thread, buffer, allocation, per-frame scan or per-sample check.
The bounded voice scan runs only when a registration's reference count reaches
zero; its physical transition cost has not been measured.

`test_audio_sample_lifetime.py` executes the real registry, original PC mixer,
decoder and extracted `hwBreak`/`hwOff`. The fixture reduces synth `voiceKill`
to its scheduled break and explicitly simulates one reentrant replacement.
It does not cover full macro/group concurrency or every stream callback.
All ten repaired subframe/no-break cases and the replacement case pass ASan/
UBSan, retain shared samples and fade-out tails, and leave an unrelated voice
playing. Four mutants fail: deferred-break-only, premature shared release,
wrong allocation selection, and stopping the callback's replacement voice.

Local normal source `2fba94b84350` /3dsx `ecf975c27546` /CIA `d116fe975b9f`
is built from `5147da16354d`, retaining its HEAT repair. The isolated build proves
